## The position (from Stage 3)

> **Biggy Smallz Spitmasters is the "Get in my belly" experience: spit braai made personal, brought to wherever your table is.** [03]

The truth underneath, which is never said out loud: the customer is buying Martin's personal attention on their event. [03]

## 1. Personality

Five traits. Each is paired with the line it must never cross.

| Trait | What it looks like | Never tips into | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Playful** | Energetic and fun, with appetite first: "Get in my belly." | Silly with the food itself | [01 §6] "energetic and fun"; [03] "Get in my belly" |
| **Serious when it's hot** | When the fire is going and the plates are going out, the fun stops and it's pure focus | Stiff or corporate when it isn't | [01 §6] "very serious when it's hot" |
| **Personal** | He calls you back himself, there are no prices online, and it comes to *your* table | Familiar with people he doesn't know | [03] "personal", "your table"; [Vault] no pricing, preserves "Martin's personal customer-conversation style" |
| **Discreet** | Private clients are never named. Work is shown by venue and occasion only | Secretive about the food or the process | [03] "per Biggy's instruction" |
| **Food-obsessed** | Food is "all he watches and almost always talks about". Influences from South Africa, Spain and Asia | Saying it (see §5), rather than showing it | [01 §5]; [Vault] food influences |

**The one that holds them together:** the same man at the plated wedding and the festival spit. The loud line and the quiet table fit together because **"Biggy makes it fit"**. The brand doesn't split into two tones. He carries both. [03, TK]

## 2. Why he's like this (internal, never said as a line)

He genuinely loves food. It's "all he watches and almost always talks about", while most caterers here are "more business focused". This is the *reason* behind the personal attention. It shows in how the work is done, and it's never used as a claim (see §5). [01 §5]

## 3. Audience

### Who walks up today, evenly split [01 §2]

| Segment | What they need from "made personal" | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Couple, plated wedding | It's "intimate, personal and flavourful", and the spit braai is plated | [01 §2, §8] |
| Construction site, board members | Food that won't embarrass anyone in front of the board | [01 §2]; [01 §6] "serious when it's hot" |
| Family milestone or birthday | Their day treated as seriously as a stranger's wedding | [01 §2] |
| Events manager: festival, concert, large gathering | A food vendor they don't have to chase | [01 §2] |

### Who it's building toward [01 §3]

**Culinary schools and institutions, for partnership and revenue.** The first real door is CTIA's Mbombela campus, which already runs "industry partnerships with luxury lodges and other establishments in the region". [02 §5] What a school would be partnering with is a working pitmaster whose record and method can be shown. [02 §3]

### The budget buyer — also welcome [TK, 2026-09-23]

Research found a market of R100–R300/person spit-braai buffets. [02 §1] Asked whether a buyer shopping on that price is someone Biggy wants, TK said: **"Yes he will take a customer like that."** No segment is turned away on budget, so the brand mustn't signal that it's out of reach. It's made personal at any size of budget. [TK, 2026-09-23]
