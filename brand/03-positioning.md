# Biggy Smallz Spitmasters — Stage 3: Positioning

*Ground · 2026-09-23 · second pass · **approved: Option A** (TK and Martin)*

> **Decided:** Biggy Smallz Spitmasters is the "Get in my belly" experience: spit braai made personal, brought to wherever your table is.
>
> On the risk that the loud line sits oddly next to an intimate plated wedding, TK said: **"Biggy makes it fit."**

The job of this stage is one sentence, in the client's own words, that answers the standing question: **what is the customer really buying?**

## Why there's a second pass

The first pass recommended *"the spitmaster who is hands-on on everything: even when he's not on site, he knows exactly what's happening."* Martin read it. He agreed it's true that he's hands-on, but said it **doesn't sound like him** and asked for that information to be **more subtle**.

So there are two changes:
1. **The words now come from Martin, not TK.** The first pass only had TK's words. Martin has spoken before, though. The August feedback pass on the live site was made "per Biggy" (commit `bb257a7`, vault note of 2026-08-24).
2. **"Hands-on" is never said out loud.** It stays as the truth underneath, and the brand shows it instead of claiming it.

**How he talks:** about the eating, not about himself. It's playful and appetite-first ("get in my belly"), it's personal ("personal", "your table"), he calls it an *experience*, and he's discreet about the people he serves. He never says anything like "hands-on" or "I oversee everything". He does the hands-on part: he calls you back himself and keeps your name private. That's how subtle it needs to be.

## What the customer is really buying (unchanged underneath)

Stage 2 still rules out spit braai alone (it's a commodity), a love of food (Umbali says it), a name alone (Philip Spitbraai) and fine dining alone (ChefMaison). What's left is still Martin's personal attention on their event. The difference now is that it's carried by one word, Martin's own word **personal**, and by the things he does, instead of being announced.

## Three options

### Option A — the experience ← **recommended**
> **Biggy Smallz Spitmasters is the "Get in my belly" experience: spit braai made personal, brought to wherever your table is.**

- All Martin's words: "Get in my belly", "experience", "personal", "brought to wherever your table is". "Spit braai" is the business itself (TK: "Spitmasters is important").
- **What it quietly says:** "personal" means one person's attention on your event, and "your table" means his care comes to you. Hands-on is in there, but it's never claimed.
- **For:** it sounds like him, because it *is* him. It's appetite-first like he is. It names the spit braai, which the name has earned, and it covers plated weddings as well as festivals. And no local competitor says anything like it.
- **Against:** "Get in my belly" is loud, and it could seem to sit oddly next to an intimate plated wedding. That's a job for the verbal identity in Stage 5 (where it's said, and where it isn't). It isn't a reason to lose it: Martin chose it over his own business name.

### Option B — his site line, as it stands
> **Personal chef service, brought to wherever your table is — from fire feasts to a 7-course tasting menu.**

- Martin's exact words, already live.
- **For:** it's 100% his, and it's already approved by him.
- **Against:** it describes the *service*, not what's bought. "Personal chef" is also ChefMaison's category. And "spit braai" isn't in it, even though TK says it's what he's known for.

### Option C — the quietest version
> **Biggy Smallz Spitmasters: spit braai, made personal.**

- Martin's word "personal", plus the thing he's known for.
- **For:** it's the most subtle of the three. It says nothing about him, and it could only be true of a one-man-led business.
- **Against:** it's so short it doesn't carry "your table" or the experience, and it loses his playfulness. It's a good candidate for a short form in Stage 5 rather than for the position.
