Biggy Smallz Spitmasters — Typography & Colour System
1. Primary display font
Oswald

Use: Canva + website display typography.

Why Oswald: It gives Biggy Smallz the condensed, high-impact headline behaviour the direction calls for, but feels more contemporary and editorial than a heavier “BBQ badge” typeface. Canva itself uses Oswald in its font-pairing guidance, alongside Montserrat, and describes the combination as a deliberate narrow/wide contrast.

It is especially suited to the brand’s existing oversized-word device:

FIRE
FEAST
BRAAI
BIG
EAT
SERVE
CHEF

Recommended weights
Oswald Bold — hero headlines, campaigns, experience names
Oswald SemiBold — subheads, supporting statements
Oswald Medium — occasional labels or smaller editorial headings
Usage

Use predominantly UPPERCASE.

Keep headlines short. Oswald works best when the words themselves are doing the heavy lifting.

Good:

GET IN MY BELLY.

Good:

THE FIRE IS ON.

Good:

SEVEN COURSES.
ONE TABLE.

Avoid long paragraphs in Oswald.

2. Secondary body font
Montserrat

Use: body copy, navigation, menus, descriptions, pricing, booking information and website UI.

Canva explicitly recommends Montserrat/Oswald as a pairing, making it a practical choice for a Canva-led identity rather than a font that needs special installation.

Montserrat brings a useful counterbalance to Oswald: wider, cleaner and calmer, while still feeling contemporary enough for a food-experience brand.

Recommended weights
Montserrat Regular — body copy
Montserrat Medium — navigation, labels, supporting information
Montserrat SemiBold — buttons, key information, short emphasis
Montserrat Bold — very limited; use for important UI or promotional emphasis
Usage

Sentence case should be the default for body copy.

Use generous line spacing and relatively short paragraphs.

Example:

Big fire. Big flavour. Biggy Smallz brings live-fire cooking, generous hospitality and a little theatre to your gathering.

This follows the approved direction of keeping website copy short, confident and conversational, rather than sounding like corporate catering copy.

3. Optional accent font
None

I would not add an accent font.

A handwritten or script face would push the brand towards rustic BBQ, lifestyle-market or “craft food” territory and introduce visual noise.

The personality should come from:

Oswald + photography + scale + cropping + colour.

That is much more ownable and practical.

Three rules for designers

1. Oswald creates impact.
2. Montserrat creates clarity.
3. Photography creates personality.

Do not use typography to manufacture personality that should already be coming from the imagery.

6. Primary colour palette

I would retain the approved palette exactly.

Colour	HEX	Primary role
Charcoal Black	#171412	Main dark background
Ember Orange	#E6531A	CTA, energy, highlights
Flame Red	#B52A18	Secondary accent
Bone White	#F3E8D5	Main light background
Ash Grey	#77716A	Secondary information

The approved application ratio is:

60% Charcoal / Bone
25% Photography
10% Ember Orange
5% Flame Red / Ash

That ratio is important. The orange and red should remain punctuation, not wallpaper.

7. Supporting & neutral colours

I would keep supporting colours extremely restrained.

Core neutrals
Charcoal Black — #171412
Bone White — #F3E8D5

These are the brand’s visual foundation.

Secondary neutral
Ash Grey — #77716A

Use for:

metadata
secondary labels
timestamps
location information
less-important UI content

Do not use Ash Grey for important body copy on Bone White; its contrast is not strong enough for comfortable accessibility.

Warm support

Use photography itself to introduce additional warmth through:

timber
skin tones
smoke
embers
food colour
ceramics
natural materials

That is preferable to expanding the palette into lots of browns and “African-inspired” earth tones, which could make the brand drift into rustic territory.

8. Colour combinations to avoid
Avoid these as standard text treatments

Flame Red text on Charcoal
Too weak for normal-sized text.

Ember Orange text on Bone White
Works for large display type, but is too low-contrast for normal body copy.

Ash Grey text on Charcoal
Not strong enough for primary reading text.

Ash Grey on Bone White
Use only for minor metadata, not body copy.

Ember Orange + Flame Red together as large areas
They are too close in value and hue. The result becomes hot, busy and less sophisticated.

Also avoid

Charcoal + orange + red + multiple coloured text blocks in one composition.

The brand will feel much stronger when one accent colour is doing the job.

9. Accessibility for text over photographs

This matters particularly because the approved direction calls for full-bleed photography and typography over photography.

For web content, WCAG AA calls for at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text.

Practical Biggy Smallz rule

Never place important copy directly over a photograph just because it looks good in the layout.

Instead:

Option A — Dark image:
Use Bone White text.

Option B — Light image:
Use Charcoal text.

Option C — Busy photograph:
Add a controlled dark or light overlay behind the text.

Option D — Uncertain contrast:
Move the text into a solid Charcoal/Bone area.

Best photographic overlay

For dark images, use a subtle Charcoal Black overlay, not a heavy black gradient that makes the photograph look artificially processed.

Important distinction

Large hero typography gives you more flexibility, but smaller website copy, navigation and buttons should still be designed for reliable contrast. WCAG specifically notes that larger text has a lower minimum contrast threshold because it is easier to distinguish.

10. The simplest colour rule

Use this as the designer’s one-line rule:

Charcoal anchors. Bone breathes. Orange activates. Red punctuates. Ash informs. Photography brings the heat.

Or, even simpler:

Dark = authority

Charcoal for major backgrounds, hero sections and strong brand statements.

Light = clarity

Bone for menus, information, copy-heavy sections and quieter layouts.

Orange = action

Use Ember Orange for buttons, calls to action, key words and moments that need attention.

Red = emphasis

Use Flame Red sparingly for small graphic accents, highlights or campaign moments.

Ash = secondary

Use Ash Grey for supporting information only.

The visual hierarchy

OSWALD = BIG STATEMENT
MONTSERRAT = EASY INFORMATION
CHARCOAL + BONE = BRAND FOUNDATION
ORANGE = ENERGY
RED = PUNCTUATION
PHOTOGRAPHY = EMOTION

That gives Biggy Smallz a system that should remain recognisable whether it is sitting on an Instagram Reel, WhatsApp flyer, wedding proposal, festival poster or website hero — without becoming a generic restaurant, rustic braai brand or over-luxury private chef identity. The approved direction explicitly prioritises simple reproduction across Instagram, WhatsApp, print and web, with authentic photography and strong, restrained typography doing the work.

One caveat: Canva’s font library is large and changing, but Canva currently documents both Oswald and Montserrat as available font choices/pairings, and its recent expansion significantly broadened the platform’s font library.
