01 — FIRE & ATTITUDE
The bold, fiery direction
1. Direction name

FIRE & ATTITUDE

2. Core idea

Put the fire, the food and the chef's personality front and centre — unapologetically big, hot and full of attitude.

This is the most recognisable expression of the "Biggy Smallz" name.

3. Emotional impression

Exciting · provocative · confident · energetic · hungry

The viewer should almost feel the heat coming off the screen.

This direction says:

"Something serious is about to come off that fire."

It should have the energy of arriving at a party when the spit is turning, smoke is in the air and everyone is waiting for the carving.

4. Colour palette
Colour	HEX
Charcoal Black	#171412
Ember Orange	#E6531A
Flame Red	#B52A18
Bone White	#F3E8D5
Ash Grey	#77716A

Colour ratio:
Black dominates. Bone provides breathing room. Orange/red are used as controlled bursts of heat.

The palette should look particularly strong against photography of flames, smoke and meat.

5. Typography direction

Big, condensed, expressive typography.

Primary:

Heavy condensed sans serif
Uppercase headlines
Tight tracking
Oversized type
Strong contrast between huge headlines and small supporting copy

Secondary:

Clean grotesk sans serif
Used for menus, descriptions, dates and practical information

Typography should feel almost poster-like.

Example:

GET
IN MY
BELLY.

Not:

An exceptional culinary experience awaits.

6. Photography direction

Photography should be close, physical and unapologetic.

Prioritise:

Flames licking meat
Smoke drifting across the frame
Fat rendering
Knife hitting meat
Hands carving
Juices and crust
Charred surfaces
Chef holding a finished cut
Guests reacting
Plates being passed
Wide shots of a packed table

Use:

Hard directional light
Deep shadows
Strong contrast
Tight crops
Occasional flash photography
Motion blur
Low angles

Don't make everything perfectly composed.

Controlled chaos is part of the appeal.

7. Video direction

Fast, energetic and sensory.

Think:

Fire → knife → meat → face → reaction → plate → fire.

Use short sequences:

Match strike
Flames rising
Spit turning
Knife slicing
Steam/smoke
Sauce hitting food
Chef looking into camera
Guests shouting/laughing
Final plate landing

Sound is important:

fire crackle + knife + sizzle + laughter + music.

Avoid overly polished restaurant commercials.

Textures should feel real, not artificially grungy.

A possible sequence:

POST 03: Chef portrait
POST 04: Knife/carving detail
POST 05: Full-table feast
POST 06: Guest reaction

Use very little copy on the image.

The caption can carry the story.

02 — THE FEAST TABLE
The warm, communal direction
1. Direction name

THE FEAST TABLE

2. Core idea

Make the gathering itself the hero — Biggy Smallz is the reason people come together, eat generously and stay longer.

This direction moves away from "look at the fire" and towards:

"Look at everyone around this table."

3. Emotional impression

Warm · generous · joyful · familiar · celebratory

This is the most emotionally accessible route.

It should feel like:

"You need to be here."

It taps into the South African cultural power of food as a reason to gather.

4. Colour palette
Colour	HEX
Terracotta	#B65A3A
Burnt Sienna	#8E402D
Warm Sand	#E5CBA8
Cream	#F5EBDD
Deep Olive	#4D5035

Compared with Direction 01, this palette is softer and more sun-baked.

It should work beautifully with natural photography.

5. Typography direction

Pair:

Warm contemporary serif + humanist sans serif

The serif gives the brand personality and hospitality.

The sans keeps it modern and practical.

Headlines can have a slightly conversational quality:

COME HUNGRY.
STAY FOR THE FEAST.

Typography should feel confident but approachable rather than aggressive.

6. Photography direction

Photography becomes people-first.

Prioritise:

Guests reaching across the table
Plates being passed
Someone carving for friends
Hands sharing food
Laughing groups
Chef interacting with guests
Children/families where appropriate
Toasts
Empty plates after the meal
Big tables
Outdoor gatherings
Sunset light

The food remains important, but food + people is the visual equation.

Use natural light wherever possible.

The photography should feel like someone was actually there.

7. Video direction

Slower and more observational.

Instead of rapid food montages:

Arrive → fire starts → guests gather → cooking → serving → eating → laughing → final moments.

Capture little human moments.

For example:

A guest takes the first bite.

Pause.

They look at the person next to them.

"Wow."

That's the brand.

8. Graphic & layout style

Warm editorial layouts with generous space.

Possible devices:

Rounded image crops
Large cream backgrounds
Terracotta blocks
Handwritten notes
Simple line illustrations
Menu-style typography
Small location/date stamps
Framed photography

The layouts should feel collected and human, not overly designed.

11. Website visual style

Bright, warm and immersive.

Hero image:

A long table filled with people and food.

Headline:

BRING THE PEOPLE.
WE'LL BRING THE FEAST.

Then introduce the experiences.

The website should feel like an invitation, rather than a digital brochure.

13. What to avoid
Generic rustic aesthetics
Farmhouse clichés
Excessive beige
Fake African patterns

Losing the chef's personality

There must still be fire and attitude underneath it.

03 — FIRE AS FINE ART
The editorial, premium direction
1. Direction name

FIRE AS FINE ART

2. Core idea

Treat fire, food and the chef as subjects of contemporary editorial photography — sophisticated enough for private dining, but still unmistakably Biggy Smallz.

This is the most elevated route.

It proves that a spitbraai brand doesn't need to look rustic to feel premium.

3. Emotional impression

Sophisticated · intriguing · confident · sensual · exclusive

The feeling is:

"This is something I haven't experienced before."

Not luxury-hotel formal.

Rather:

cultured, contemporary, confident and quietly provocative.

4. Colour palette
Colour	HEX
Obsidian	#111111
Oat	#D8CCB8
Parchment	#EFE8DA
Burnished Copper	#9A6245
Deep Forest	#26372F

The copper is important.

It provides a subtle connection to fire without resorting to obvious orange.

5. Typography direction

This direction gets the most sophisticated typographic treatment.

Primary

High-contrast contemporary serif

Used for:

Campaign headlines
Experience names
Editorial statements
Secondary

Minimalist grotesk sans

Used for:

Navigation
Descriptions
Menus
Information

Typography should create a tension between:

old-world culinary culture + contemporary South African design.

6. Photography direction

Photography becomes almost art photography.

Think:

Meat against dark backgrounds
Smoke floating through light
Chef portraits
Extreme macro food details
Sculptural plating
Hands
Knives
Fire reflected in metal
Dramatic table settings
Negative space
Architectural compositions

Use intentional lighting.

Not every photograph needs to show the whole dish.

A photograph of a knife, smoke and a sliver of charred meat can be more powerful than a conventional food shot.

7. Video direction

Slow, cinematic and sensual.

Longer shots.

Minimal editing.

Sound design becomes important:

knife → crackle → sizzle → silence → glass → laughter.

A campaign film could open on complete darkness.

Then:

strike of a match.

Fire appears.

Cut to the chef.

Cut to meat.

Cut to a table.

Then:

GET IN MY BELLY.

This route gives Biggy Smallz room to create genuinely premium campaign films.

Strong grids
Elegant typography
Full-bleed photography
Small captions
Asymmetric editorial layouts
Fine rules
Large serif headlines
Restrained colour

Think food magazine + contemporary cultural publication, rather than restaurant menu.

The design should know when to stop.

13. What to avoid

This is where the premium direction could go wrong.

Avoid:

Gold everywhere
Champagne imagery

Pretentious culinary language
French restaurant visual codes

The rule is:

Premium through art direction, not luxury clichés.

The brand should still smell like fire and food.

My strategic recommendation

I would not choose one direction and discard the others completely.

The strongest Biggy Smallz identity could take:

The visual attitude of Direction 01
the human warmth of Direction 02
the art direction discipline of Direction 03

In other words:

Bold enough to stop the scroll. Warm enough to make people want to join the table. Sophisticated enough to sell a seven-course private dining experience.

That combination directly supports the existing strategic principle that the brand should be "premium without being precious" and should retain its personal chef character rather than becoming an overly polished corporate catering brand.

If I were directing the identity, I'd make Direction 01 — FIRE & ATTITUDE the primary creative territory, use Direction 02 — THE FEAST TABLE as the emotional/social layer, and borrow the editorial restraint of Direction 03 for private dining, tasting menus and higher-end brand communications.
