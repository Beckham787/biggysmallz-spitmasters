FINAL CREATIVE DIRECTION
1. Creative direction name
FIRE & ATTITUDE
Internal creative principle:

Make it feel like Biggy Smallz just arrived.

2. One-sentence concept

A bold South African visual world where fire, food, chef personality and real human reactions collide to make every gathering feel like a Biggy Smallz occasion.

3. Brand mood
Bold

Never timid.

Hot

Visually and emotionally driven by fire.

Human

Real people, real food, real reactions.

Generous

Abundance should be visible.

Theatrical

Cooking and serving are part of the show.

Confident

Never needs to shout that it is premium.

South African

Rooted in local gathering and braai culture without resorting to clichés.

The overall feeling:

"This looks like the party I want to be at."

4. Colour palette

I'd retain the original Fire & Attitude palette but make the application more disciplined.

Colour	HEX	Role
Charcoal Black	#171412	Primary background
Ember Orange	#E6531A	Energy / CTA / highlights
Flame Red	#B52A18	Accent
Bone White	#F3E8D5	Primary light background
Ash Grey	#77716A	Supporting information
Application

60% Charcoal / Bone
25% photography
10% Ember Orange
5% Flame Red / Ash

The orange and red should be punctuation, not wallpaper.

5. Typography direction
Primary: Bold condensed sans serif

Use it for:

headlines
campaign statements
event titles
experience names
large numbers

Characteristics:

Heavy · condensed · uppercase · confident · tight

Example:

FIRE.
FOOD.
PEOPLE.
Secondary: Neutral grotesk sans serif

Use it for:

menus
descriptions
website navigation
pricing
locations
booking information
Important rule

Don't introduce five different typefaces to make it feel "creative".

Two type families are enough.

The photography and headline scale create the personality.

6. Photography rules

Photography is arguably the most important part of the identity.

Rule 1 — Photograph the real experience

No stock photography.

Ever.

Rule 2 — Food must look edible

Prioritise:

crust
smoke
juices
char
texture
steam
carving
hands
serving

Don't over-retouch food until it looks plastic.

Rule 3 — Photograph the chef as a character

Not just:

"professional chef portrait."

Instead:

looking at camera
working
laughing
carving
sweating
talking to guests
holding food
interacting with the fire
Rule 4 — Show reactions

A guest's face after tasting something can be more valuable than another food close-up.

Rule 5 — Shoot wide as well as tight

The visual rhythm should move:

MACRO → PERSON → FOOD → FIRE → TABLE → CROWD

Rule 6 — Keep some imperfection

Smoke obscuring part of a photograph is okay.

A hand entering the frame is okay.

Motion blur is okay.

A slightly messy table is okay.

Real beats perfect.

Rule 7 — Use strong light

Prefer:

directional sunlight
firelight
flash
hard shadows
evening light
dramatic backlight

Avoid the generic bright-white restaurant aesthetic.

7. Video rules

Video should be simple enough to produce constantly.

Don't build the brand around expensive commercials.

The Biggy sequence:

FIRE → FOOD → CHEF → PEOPLE → REACTION

A 10-second Reel can be enough.

Capture:
fire being lit
spit turning
meat hitting the grill
smoke
knife slicing
carving
serving
guest reaction
chef laughing
full table
empty plates
Editing

Use:

quick cuts for fire content
longer shots for emotional moments
natural sound
music where appropriate
occasional text overlays
Golden rule

Don't make every video a food montage.

Sometimes the strongest video is simply:

Chef looks into camera.

"You hungry?"

Cut to fire.

8. Layout rules

The design language should be big, asymmetric and confident.

Do:
Oversized typography
Full-bleed photography
Cropped images
Black/cream fields
Strong alignment
Large margins
Occasional graphic blocks
Typography over photography
Unexpected crops
Simple information hierarchy
Don't:
Put everything inside boxes
Use endless rounded cards
Centre everything
Overdecorate
Use excessive patterns
Make every post look identical
Signature device

Use oversized single words:

FIRE
FEAST
BRAAI
BIG
EAT
SERVE
CHEF

This becomes an ownable visual shorthand.

9. Social-media rules

Instagram and WhatsApp should feel like the same brand in different clothes.

Content ratio

I'd recommend roughly:

40% food/fire
25% people/experience
20% chef/personality
10% offers/events
5% brand/editorial

The feed should not become an endless catalogue of dishes.

Instagram

Use:

Reels
carousels
food close-ups
chef content
event recaps
guest reactions
occasional typography posts
WhatsApp

Make assets simpler.

For example:

FIRE FEAST THIS SATURDAY.

Food photograph.

BOOK YOUR EXPERIENCE

Don't try to reproduce complicated Instagram layouts.

Social rule

Every piece of content should make someone either hungry, curious or want to be there.

Ideally two of the three.

10. Website rules

The website should behave like a food experience, not a catering brochure.

Homepage opening

Full-screen real image or short video.

Headline:

GET IN MY BELLY.

Supporting copy:

Fire. Food. People. Let's make a feast.

CTA:

BOOK YOUR EXPERIENCE

Then immediately show the three core experiences:

FIRE FEASTS
PRIVATE DINING
SEVEN-COURSE EXPERIENCES

The strategic recommendation is already to use Book Your Experience as the main online action rather than treating Biggy Smallz like an ordinary food-ordering business.

Website photography

Prioritise real event photography over graphic decoration.

Website tone

Short.

Confident.

Conversational.

No paragraphs of corporate catering jargon.

11. Three campaign concepts
Campaign 01 — GET IN MY BELLY

The hero brand campaign.

Visual idea

Extreme food close-ups, chef personality and guest reactions.

Copy:

GET IN MY BELLY.

Then:

Fire feasts. Private dining. Seven courses.
Whatever the occasion, come hungry.

This makes the existing tagline a visual brand asset rather than simply a slogan. The strategy already identifies it as distinctive and worth retaining.

Campaign 02 — THE FIRE IS ON

An ongoing content campaign.

Concept

Document the transformation:

Cold → Fire → Feast

Each event becomes content.

Examples:

THE FIRE IS ON.
THE SPIT IS TURNING.
THE KNIFE IS OUT.
THE TABLE IS FULL.

This is highly practical because it can be created from normal event photography and phone footage.

Campaign 03 — WHO'S COMING HUNGRY?

A social-first campaign focused on people.

Concept

Biggy Smallz asks:

"Who's coming hungry?"

Then show:

weddings
birthdays
corporate events
festivals
private dinners
friends
families
chefs
guests

It makes the brand about participation, not just food.

13. Visual clichés to avoid

This is a critical part of the direction.

Avoid generic BBQ clichés
Cartoon flames
Flame illustrations
Smoking skulls
BBQ badges
Grillmaster logos
American flag motifs
Vintage Americana
Fake butcher-shop branding
Excessive woodgrain
Avoid generic African clichés
Safari imagery
Animal prints
Tribal-pattern backgrounds
Generic African masks
Baobab silhouettes
Overused earth-tone palettes
Decorative "African" patterns with no meaningful connection

The existing strategy specifically cautions against turning "African" into safari imagery or fake patterns.

Avoid generic premium clichés
Black + gold everything
Champagne glasses
Marble tables
Excessive candles
Pretentious plating
Hotel-lobby photography
Corporate-looking chef portraits
Avoid generic social clichés
Inspirational quotes
"Food is our passion"
"Where taste meets excellence"
Excessive Canva graphics
Stock photos
Over-designed menus
Every image surrounded by a coloured border
Most importantly:

Don't make Biggy Smallz look like someone else's idea of a braai brand.

14. Designer consistency paragraph

The designer should treat Fire & Attitude as a flexible visual system rather than a fixed template. Every execution should start with something real — fire, food, the chef, guests or the table — and then use bold typography, strong cropping, controlled colour and confident composition to amplify that moment. Keep the palette predominantly charcoal and bone, using ember orange and flame red sparingly for energy. Photography should always feel immediate and human, while layouts should remain simple enough to reproduce quickly across Instagram, WhatsApp, print and web. When in doubt, choose the more authentic photograph, the simpler layout and the stronger headline. The brand should always feel like Biggy Smallz has just walked into the room with the fire already going.

Final creative-director verdict

Fire & Attitude is the right foundation because it has the strongest relationship between the brand name, the actual product and the personality of the business.

But the mature version isn't:

"LOUD BBQ BRAND."

It's:

BOLD SOUTH AFRICAN FOOD EXPERIENCE.

That distinction matters.

The fire gives it recognisability.
The food gives it appetite appeal.
The chef gives it personality.
The people give it warmth.
The editorial discipline gives it credibility.

That combination gives Biggy Smallz room to sell a festival spitbraai on Saturday and a seven-course private dinner on Tuesday without looking like two different businesses. The strategy's core ambition — to move beyond ordinary catering into a memorable food-experience brand — is therefore preserved.
