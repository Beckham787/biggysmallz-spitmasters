# 4. FONT SIZES

Keep the hierarchy simple.

## Page title

**Oswald Bold**

**32–40 pt**

Use approximately **36 pt** as the default.

Examples:

**BRAND ESSENCE**

**PHOTOGRAPHY**

**TYPOGRAPHY**

---

## Hero headline

**Oswald Bold**

**52–76 pt**

Use approximately **64 pt** as the starting point.

Examples:

**GET IN MY BELLY.**

**BIG FIRE.  
BIG FLAVOUR.**

For particularly strong words:

**FIRE**

can go much larger.

---

## Subheading

**Oswald SemiBold**

**20–26 pt**

Use approximately **23 pt** as the default.

---

## Body copy

**Montserrat Regular**

**10.5–13 pt**

Use approximately **11.5–12 pt**.

Do not make body copy unnecessarily large.

---

## Body emphasis

**Montserrat SemiBold**

**11.5–13 pt**

Use for:

- key phrases
- important statements
- labels
- short emphasis

---

## Buttons / CTA

**Montserrat SemiBold**

**11–14 pt**

Uppercase.

Examples:

**BOOK YOUR EXPERIENCE**

**ENQUIRE NOW**

---

## Small labels

**Montserrat Medium**

**8–10 pt**

Use for:

- page numbers
- captions
- colour codes
- minor notes
- metadata

### Typography rule

Do not use more than **four meaningful text sizes on one page**.

The approved direction relies on scale and hierarchy rather than multiple typefaces or excessive graphic treatment.

# 5. MARGIN & SPACING RULES

Create one invisible grid and use it everywhere.

### Outer page margin

Use:

**18–20 mm**

from every edge.

A good default:

**20 mm**

### Image bleed

When an image is intended to be full-bleed:

**Extend it to the edge of the page.**

Do not leave an accidental white strip.

### Text spacing

Use approximately:

**8–12 mm** between major text sections.

### Paragraph spacing

Use:

**4–6 mm**

between short paragraphs.

### Line spacing

For body copy:

**1.25–1.4**

For large headlines:

**0.85–1.0**

Tight headline spacing helps create the bold editorial feel.

### Important rule

Do not fill every available space.

**Large margins are part of the design.**

The approved direction specifically calls for **large margins, strong alignment and simple information hierarchy**.

# 6. IMAGE TREATMENT RULES

Photography is the most important visual ingredient.

## Use real photography only

Prioritise actual Biggy Smallz:

- food
- fire
- chef
- guests
- tables
- events

No stock photography.

No fake event photography.

No AI-generated imagery presented as actual Biggy Smallz work.

The approved direction explicitly puts **real experience and real food** at the centre of the identity.

---

## Image selection

Choose photographs with:

**Fire**  
**Texture**  
**Movement**  
**Human interaction**  
**Strong expressions**  
**Visible food detail**

Avoid photographs where:

- everyone is staring at camera
- food is flatly lit
- colours are washed out
- the subject is too small
- the image looks like generic catering photography

---

## Cropping

Be confident.

Crop tightly into:

- meat
- flames
- hands
- faces
- knives
- platters

Do not be afraid to crop part of the subject out.

---

## Editing

Keep editing consistent:

- slightly warm
- strong contrast
- natural skin tones
- visible food texture
- controlled highlights
- deep but detailed shadows

Avoid heavy filters.

Avoid orange-tinted everything.

Avoid excessive HDR.

---

## Canva image rule

When placing type over photography:

**Dark image → Bone White type**

**Light image → Charcoal type**

For busy images, add a subtle overlay or move the text onto a solid field.

# 7. COLOUR APPLICATION RULES

Use the approved palette:

### CHARCOAL BLACK
**#171412**

Main dark background.

### BONE WHITE
**#F3E8D5**

Main light background.

### EMBER ORANGE
**#E6531A**

CTA / highlight / energy.

### FLAME RED
**#B52A18**

Small accent.

### ASH GREY
**#77716A**

Supporting information.

---

## Simple application rule

**CHARCOAL = anchor**

Use it for:

- dark pages
- major statements
- hero backgrounds
- strong transitions

**BONE = breathe**

Use it for:

- body-copy pages
- editorial layouts
- information
- quieter moments

**ORANGE = activate**

Use it for:

- CTAs
- important highlights
- occasional key words
- graphic accents

**RED = punctuate**

Use sparingly.

**ASH = inform**

Use only for secondary information.

The approved ratio is approximately:

**60% Charcoal / Bone**  
**25% Photography**  
**10% Ember Orange**  
**5% Flame Red / Ash**

### Golden rule

Do not use orange and red simply because they are available.

**The accents should feel earned.**

# 9. HOW TO CREATE CONSISTENT PAGE LAYOUTS

Don't design 12 completely different pages.

Create **four master layouts** and reuse them.

## LAYOUT A — HERO

Use for:

**Cover / major statements / closing page**

Structure:

**FULL-BLEED IMAGE**

Large Oswald headline.

Small supporting copy.

CTA if required.

---

## LAYOUT B — IMAGE + COPY

Use for:

**Brand Essence / Story / Audience / Photography**

Structure:

**IMAGE 55%**  
**COPY 45%**

or reverse the arrangement.

---

## LAYOUT C — EDITORIAL

Use for:

**Personality / Colour / Typography / Content**

Structure:

Large headline.

Short blocks of information.

Generous empty space.

Small graphic accents.

---

## LAYOUT D — APPLICATION

Use for:

**Brand in Use**

Show actual examples:

Instagram  
WhatsApp  
Website  
Event artwork

---

## Consistency trick

Keep these things fixed across the entire guide:

**Same margin**  
**Same title position**  
**Same page-number position**  
**Same font hierarchy**  
**Same colour logic**  
**Same image treatment**

Then vary the photography and composition.

This gives the document a designed system rather than 12 unrelated Canva pages.

# 11. FINAL QUALITY-CONTROL CHECKLIST

Before exporting the brand guide, check every page.

## BRAND

☐ Does this look unmistakably Biggy Smallz?

☐ Does the page feel bold, hot, human and confident?

☐ Does it feel like a South African food experience rather than a generic catering company?

---

## TYPOGRAPHY

☐ Is Oswald used for impact?

☐ Is Montserrat used for readability?

☐ Are headings consistently sized?

☐ Are there only two type families?

☐ Are headlines short enough to remain powerful?

☐ Is body copy easy to read?

---

## COLOUR

☐ Is Charcoal/Bone doing most of the work?

☐ Is Orange used as an accent rather than a background?

☐ Is Red used sparingly?

☐ Is Ash Grey only supporting information?

☐ Does each page have a clear colour hierarchy?

---

## PHOTOGRAPHY

☐ Is the image real Biggy Smallz photography?

☐ Does the food look genuinely appetising?

☐ Is there visible texture?

☐ Is the chef shown as a character?

☐ Are people shown naturally?

☐ Are photographs cropped confidently?

☐ Are images free from excessive filters?

The visual direction prioritises **authenticity, food texture, chef personality, reactions and controlled imperfection**.

---

## LAYOUT

☐ Are margins consistent?

☐ Is there enough empty space?

☐ Is the hierarchy obvious within three seconds?

☐ Is the layout asymmetric where appropriate?

☐ Is the page free from unnecessary boxes?

☐ Are there no excessive patterns or decorative elements?

☐ Does the page still look strong if some text is removed?

---

## CONTENT

☐ Is the copy concise?

☐ Is the language conversational?

☐ Is anything overly corporate?

☐ Is anything trying too hard to sound luxurious?

☐ Are all business facts accurate?

☐ Are missing details marked **[PLACEHOLDER]**?

☐ Are no invented awards, clients, testimonials, dates or prices included?

---

## REALITY CHECK

Before finalising the guide, ask:

**Could this page have been made from the real Biggy Smallz business?**

If the answer is no, simplify it.

The brand is strongest when the design amplifies reality rather than creating a fantasy around it.

---

# FINAL CANVA BUILD RULE

When in doubt, use this formula:

### **ONE STRONG PHOTO**
+
### **ONE STRONG HEADLINE**
+
### **ONE CLEAR MESSAGE**
+
### **ONE ACCENT COLOUR**

That is enough.

The approved creative direction specifically calls for **simple information hierarchy, oversized typography, strong cropping, full-bleed photography and controlled colour**, rather than complex decoration.

### THE BIGGY TEST

Before a page is approved, ask:

**Does it feel real?**

**Does it feel hungry?**

**Does it feel confident?**

**Does it feel like Biggy Smallz just arrived?**

If all four answers are yes, the page is doing its job.
